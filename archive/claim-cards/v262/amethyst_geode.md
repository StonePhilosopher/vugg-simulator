## Scenario notes (author's own rationale)
> Anchor: Ametista do Sul, Rio Grande do Sul — the classic Brazilian amethyst-geode district, hosted in a ~40-50 m subhorizontal high-Ti basalt flow of the Serra Geral Formation (Paraná flood basalts, Lower Cretaceous ~134 Ma). Geode structure INWARD: celadonite rim → agate/chalcedony → colourless quartz → amethyst (Gilg et al. 2003).

> Formation temperature is genuinely debated in the literature and the scenario bridges the two camps. Monophase liquid inclusions with low ice-melting depressions argue a LOW-T, low-salinity fluid (<100°C, probably <50°C — a sedimentary-brine / paleo-aquifer model; Gilg et al. 2003). Primary inclusions in the amethyst itself yield 152-238°C with a MAGMATIC silica source (Triz-quarry study, Geol. Mag. 144:731). The scenario holds the early agate/quartz/amethyst stage near 160°C through step 190, then cools to ~70°C by step 240 for the celadonite/goethite overprint. This stages the two measured temperature populations instead of averaging them into one continuous ambiguous ramp.

> AMETHYST COLOUR is Fe³⁺ substituting for Si⁴⁺, activated by a natural γ-dose (Rossman 1994). The Serra Geral basalt is mafic — low K/U/Th per unit time — but the geode has sat in it for ~134 Ma, so its integrated background dose tips the Fe centres to amethyst (NOT the granite-hosted morion of a Grimsel cleft). wall.gamma_host=0.23 is the normalized long-residence integrated dose, not an instantaneous U-Th assay; over the longer warm growth stage it lands radiation_damage in the amethyst band (>0.1, <0.3). Every other basalt scenario leaves gamma_host unset and receives no such long-residence dose.

> THE SILICA PHASE SEQUENCE IS EXECUTED: the early 320 ppm fluid is above the temperature-dependent chalcedony equilibrium and builds a real accepted-shell agate lining. At step 18, solution aging/depletion lowers dissolved SiO2 beneath the fresh-chalcedony nucleation barrier while keeping quartz supersaturated; chalcedony stops and inward euhedral quartz nucleates. This is the same T-SiO2 window a player can reproduce in Creative Mode, not a scenario-only phase flag.

> THE MASKING SCEPTRE: the celadonite (green K-Fe mica-clay) that rims these geodes also settles on the growing amethyst as a film. A partial, PRISM-dominant celadonite dusting (prism 0.18, term 0.04 — frosts gaps across the {10-10} prism, largely spares the tip) arrests the sides; these coverages keep the calibrated σ*(φ) barrier below the independently calculated chalcedony/quartz solubility ratio at 160°C. The Quartz-Window Renewal can therefore cross the film barrier while remaining below the fresh-chalcedony nucleation threshold, and the tip renews a wider {10-11}-capped termination THROUGH the film — a masking-route sceptre (classifyQuartzSceptre js/45; Takahashi & Sunagawa 2004 ELO). grimsel stays the clean CORROSION-sceptre reference; this is the non-corrosive masking counterpart. The renderer widens the cap from a length fraction and does not calculate or claim conserved solid mass.

> The late iron-oxide film is the diagnostic base staining — a termination/uniform film (prism 0.3, term 0.3), NOT a second sceptre; real Brazilian points are commonly goethite/hematite-stained at the matrix contact.
