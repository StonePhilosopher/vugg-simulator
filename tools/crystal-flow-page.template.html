Vugg Simulator · mineral catalogue

Every crystal, and what it needs to exist

All species the simulator can grow, arranged by chemistry class, with the dependencies the engine actually enforces drawn as chords through the hub. These are gate conditions read out of the nucleation code — not paragenetic folklore.

What a chord means — the state the gate demands of the earlier crystal

How the chords were found. Each nucleation engine (js/80–js/91) tests the crystals already in the vug before it will seed a new one. Every one of those tests was read and classified by the state it demands: c.active means the precursor must be present (substrate); c.dissolved means it must be breaking down (replaces); both means either will do; a bare count means mere presence. A reference is attributed to the nearest preceding gate marker, because several functions gate more than one species in a single body.

What was left out, and why. Of mineral comparisons in those files, became chords. were self-references — a species counting its own crystals, which is growth bookkeeping rather than a relation. Exactly one could not be attributed: c.mineral === 'serpentine' in the diopside gate, an alternative that can never match because no such species exists in the catalogue and nothing nucleates it. The chrysotile alternative beside it carries the logic, so the disjunct is dead rather than harmful.

Read the isolated rim with care. A species with no chords is not unreachable — it simply answers to broth chemistry, temperature and redox alone, with no other crystal named in its gate.
