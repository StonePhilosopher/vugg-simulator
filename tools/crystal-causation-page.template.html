Where crystals prefer to sit

Read left to right. These arrows record preference and opportunity, not requirement — nearly every species here has been observed nucleating on the bare wall, so its position in a column is a habit, not a prerequisite. Columns show how far a relationship chain can be followed, which is why the numbers below say reachable and never depends.

What a line means — the state the gate names for the earlier crystal

Why two modes. An earlier version of this chart drew "must have" and "likes to sit on" as one arrow and then computed roots, depth and reach over the mixture. Those numbers described a dependency structure that does not exist. Splitting the modes is not presentation — it is the correction.

The bare-wall glyph is evidence, not inference. Every scenario was run at the canonical seed and the position string of each crystal read. A species seen on the bare wall has a proved escape route, so its substrate arrows are preferences. A species never seen there is marked unresolved and nothing is claimed: absence of an observation is not a gate. Only two edges in the entire engine set actually block.

What is not here. Epitaxy as distinct from generic substrate preference, mandatory versus merely available replacement, competition and inhibition, and ion-transfer receipts are all absent because the simulator does not yet represent them separately. They are not shown as empty checkboxes, because an unticked box reads as "none" when the honest answer is "not yet measured".
